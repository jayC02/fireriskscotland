Short-term lets in Edinburgh

Edinburgh has one of the highest concentrations of short-term lets in Scotland, and two sets of rules apply to them. Every short-term let needs a licence from the City of Edinburgh Council, with fire safety as a mandatory condition. Separately, the whole council area has been a short-term let control area since 5 September 2022, so using a property that is not your main home as a short-term let needs planning permission.

Our short-term let assessments give you the fire risk assessment the licence condition requires, with clear actions and the guest fire safety information you need to display.

Listed and historic buildings

Many Edinburgh premises are in listed buildings in the Old Town and New Town. The Scottish Government's fire safety guidance recognises that, in a listed building, alternatives to conventional fire safety measures may be appropriate. Original doors, panelling and stairs can often be kept, with the risk managed through upgraded detection, careful fire stopping, or management measures instead. We look for proportionate solutions that protect people without unnecessary damage to the building, and we flag where listed building consent may be needed for any work.

Festival season

Occupancy in guest houses, short-term lets and venues rises sharply in August. If your premises will be used differently or by more people during the festivals, the fire risk assessment should reflect that, so book a review well before the season starts.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a fire risk assessment for an Edinburgh short-term let licence?

Yes. Fire safety is a mandatory condition of every short-term let licence in Scotland, and fire safety law requires the person in charge of the accommodation to carry out a fire risk assessment and keep it under review. Planning permission in the Edinburgh control area is a separate requirement.

How much is a fire risk assessment in Edinburgh?

From £225 + VAT for smaller premises such as a holiday let, HMO flat or small office. Larger premises, hotels and listed buildings with complex layouts are quoted individually.

Can you assess a listed building without recommending changes that harm it?

Yes. Scottish Government guidance allows alternatives to conventional measures in listed buildings. We look for proportionate solutions, such as improved detection or management measures, and flag anything that may need listed building consent.
