  1. Fire detection and warning

    We check the type and coverage of smoke and heat detection against what the premises needs, whether alarms are interlinked where required, and whether testing and servicing are recorded. Since February 2022 every home in Scotland must have interlinked smoke and heat alarms. HMOs, hotels and workplaces usually need more.

  2. Emergency lighting

    Would the escape routes still be lit if the power failed? We check fittings are in the right places and working, and look for records of monthly function tests and annual full-duration tests.

  3. Compartmentation and fire stopping

    Visual checks for gaps where pipes and cables pass through fire-resisting walls and floors, missing ceiling tiles, and alterations that could let fire and smoke spread. Assessments are non-destructive unless intrusive inspection is agreed in advance.

  4. Fire doors

    Self-closers, gaps around the leaf, intumescent strips and smoke seals, hinges, glazing and signage. Fire doors wedged open or damaged are among the most common findings in Scottish premises.

  5. Escape routes

    Travel distances, whether stairs and corridors are protected and kept clear of storage and ignition sources, alternative routes, and how people with limited mobility would get out.

  6. Ignition sources

    Cooking, heating, electrical installations and appliances, charging of e-bikes and scooters, smoking, and deliberate fire setting, including bins stored against the building.

  7. Firefighting equipment

    Whether extinguishers and fire blankets suit the risks, are in the right places and are serviced, and whether staff know when, and when not, to use them.

  8. Final exits and signage

    Exit doors that open easily without a key, fire exit signs where they are needed, and a safe route away from the building to a place of safety.

Which fire risk assessment do you need?

What the law requires, and what your council or insurer will ask for, depends on how the building is used. Choose your type of premises.

HMO fire risk assessments

A house in multiple occupation needs a licence under the Housing (Scotland) Act 2006 when three or more unrelated people from three or more families share it as their main home. Once licensed, it is a "relevant premises" under Part 3 of the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005, so the licence holder must assess the fire risk.

Councils take fire safety into account when deciding licence applications, and many ask to see the assessment, or a declaration that a competent person carried it out.

Short-term let fire risk assessments

Every short-term let in Scotland needs a licence from the local council, and fire safety is one of the mandatory licence conditions that apply everywhere. Under fire safety law the person in charge of the accommodation must carry out a fire risk assessment, act on it and keep it under review.

That covers whole-property holiday lets, B&Bs and guest houses, rooms let in your own home, and serviced apartments.

Workplace and commercial fire risk assessments

Employers, and anyone who controls non-domestic premises, must assess the fire risk to employees and to anyone else on or near the premises: customers, visitors and contractors.

Since 1 July 2023 the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service has not attended automatic fire alarm calls at most workplaces unless a fire is confirmed. Your assessment and procedures need to cover how an alarm is investigated and who calls 999.

Hotels, guest houses and care homes

People are most vulnerable to fire when they are asleep. Hotels, guest houses, hostels, student accommodation and care homes need closer attention to detection, compartmentation, fire doors, night-time staffing and the evacuation strategy.

Care homes also need evacuation planning built around each resident's needs, because some residents cannot get out unaided.

Blocks of flats and common areas

Private flats are domestic premises, but the common parts of many blocks still need assessing. The Scottish Government's guidance for high-rise domestic buildings calls for a specific fire safety risk assessment, and factors and housing providers commission them routinely.

Mixed-use buildings, such as tenements with shops on the ground floor, bring parts of the building under Part 3 of the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005.

Scottish fire law in numbers

Scotland has its own fire safety law. Part 3 of the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005 and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006 apply here, not the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order that covers England and Wales. The person responsible is the duty holder, and hiring an assessor does not pass that responsibility on.

Common questions

Is a fire risk assessment a legal requirement in Scotland?

Yes, for most non-domestic premises. Part 3 of the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005 requires employers and anyone with control of "relevant premises" to carry out a fire safety risk assessment and keep it under review. Relevant premises include workplaces, shops, hotels and guest houses, care homes, licensed HMOs and short-term lets. Private homes are not covered by Part 3.

How much does a fire risk assessment cost?

Prices start from £225 + VAT for smaller premises such as a small office, shop, HMO or holiday let. Larger or more complex buildings, such as hotels, care homes or blocks of flats, are quoted individually once we know the size and layout.

How quickly will I get the report?

Your written report is issued within 48 hours of the site visit. If something needs immediate attention, Raj tells you on the day rather than waiting for the report.

How often should a fire risk assessment be reviewed?

The Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006 require it to be reviewed regularly so it stays up to date, and whenever there is reason to think it is no longer valid or there has been a significant change. Many duty holders review annually, and higher-risk premises often review more often. Your report gives a recommended review date.

Who is responsible for fire safety if I hire an assessor?

You are. The duty holder remains legally responsible even when someone else carries out the assessment. That is why the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service recommends checking that your assessor is on a professional register or certified by a UKAS-accredited body. Raj Chall is listed on the Institution of Fire Engineers register.

Do you cover the whole of Scotland?

Yes. We are based in Glasgow and carry out assessments across Scotland, including Edinburgh, Aberdeen, Dundee, Inverness, Stirling, Perth and the Highlands and islands. Remote and island locations are quoted individually.
